## Boyko Nikolov and Marco Tesoro (Planqc)

## From Neutral Atoms to Useful Quantum Computing: Hardware and Algorithm Co-Design at planqc

Neutral atoms are a promising platform for scalable quantum computing, combining precise optical control, flexible qubit architectures and strong Rydberg interactions. Turning this potential into useful computation requires progress on two fronts: hardware that scales reliably, and algorithms designed for the machines that hardware will provide. We will show how planqc develops the two together.
<br><br>
On the hardware side, we will explain why we work with two-electron atoms, strontium and ytterbium, whose clock transitions and metastable qubits enable velocity-selective operations and erasure conversion. We will present results from the group of our Chief Science Officer, Prof. Johannes Zeiher, feed directly into our current and future machines: optical lattices of more than 10,000 atoms, two-qubit gate fidelities of up to 99.86%, and atom transport for efficient error correction.
On the algorithms side, we will take logistics optimisation as a case study to compare the widely used QUBO formulation, in which constraints are imposed through penalty terms, with constraint-aware approaches that respect them by construction. We will show how our in-house quantum-inspired tensor-network methods already allow us to identify bottlenecks in quantum routines on classical hardware, and what this complementary approach teaches us about designing algorithms for the devices we are building. A quantum-use case relevant in material science will also be presented.
<br><br>
Finally, we will present our growing R&D activities in the UK and the multidisciplinary expertise needed to take quantum computing from the lab to real-world application
