## Ayush Agrawal (University of Oxford)

##  A network of <sup>43</sup>Ca<sup>+</sup> optical clocks for entanglement-enhanced metrology

Over the past few decades, advancements in optical ion clocks have enabled
measurements of time and frequency with unprecedented precision and accuracy,
achieving systematic uncertainties at the 10−19 level [1, 2]. Precision
frequency comparisons between macroscopically separated clocks have applications
in geodesy [3], probing variations in fundamental constants, and in dark
matter searches [4]. However, ion clocks usually have only a small numbers
of ions, limiting their 1 s stability to be orders of magnitude lower than their
systematic uncertainties [2]. One can try to improve the measurement stability
by distributing entanglement across the clock network, which enables us to go
beyond the standard quantum limit (SQL) and approach the Heisenberg limit.
<br><br>
We previously demonstrated a proof-of-principle enhancement in a differential
frequency measurement across a network of two entangled optical 88Sr+
clocks [5]; however, its stability was severely limited by its sensitivity to magnetic
field fluctuations. We have now set up the next generation of this experiment
with entangled 43Ca+ ion clocks. I will present preliminary results from
this experiment, where we leverage a field-insensitive transition to extend our
Ramsey probe durations to be comparable to the 580 ms excited-state lifetime
limit. This provides genuine entanglement-enhancement, even when accounting
for the experimental overhead of generating remote entanglement.
